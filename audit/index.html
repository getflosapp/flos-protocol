What this audit actually measures

Your score does not grade your body. It measures whether the behaviors that drive results are visible, explicit, and reviewable — across the four pillars the FLOS system is built on. Every pillar below is anchored to published evidence, not opinion.

PILLAR 01 · FLOW

Energy, focus & readiness

Do you know your daily energy, and do you set one clear intent before the day starts? Consistent self-monitoring is one of the most replicated predictors of successful weight and behavior outcomes — people who measure, change more. [9]

PILLAR 02 · LOAD

Training, effort & progression

Progressive overload — measurably harder sessions over weeks — is the core variable behind continued adaptation in position-stand guidelines. [5] Logging RPE after training is a validated method to autoregulate resistance work. [6]

PILLAR 03 · OUTPUT

Calories, macros & compliance

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation predicted resting metabolic rate within 10% of measured values in more adults than Harris–Benedict, Owen, or WHO formulas. [1][2] Protein intakes around 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day maximize resistance-training gains in meta-analysis. [3][4]

PILLAR 04 · SUSTAIN

Sleep, hydration & weekly review

Adults should sleep 7 or more hours nightly — habitual short sleep is associated with weight gain and impaired performance, per the joint AASM/SRS consensus. [7] Adequate total water intake is ≈2.7–3.7 L/day from all sources. [8]

THE FLOS METHOD

Why targets beat tips

Most fitness content hands you information with no feedback loop: a tip cannot tell you whether it worked — a number can. That is why this audit does not dispense generic advice. It measures whether the behaviors that actually move results are tracked inside your week, then calculates starting targets from validated equations. [1][2][3]

The second difference is the review. Every target is an estimate — a good one [1], but an estimate. The FLOS method pairs each target with a scheduled weekly review, so the plan adapts to your own records instead of pretending a formula knows your body better than four weeks of your data does. Self-monitoring plus adjustment is where adherence actually comes from. [9]

No hacks. No "one weird trick." No fabricated before-and-afters. Visibility, structure, iteration — the unglamorous machinery behind every transformation worth trusting.

Questions, answered straight

Is the FLOS Readiness Audit really free?
Yes. The audit is a free educational self-assessment. You answer 14 questions, enter your email to unlock your full pillar breakdown instantly on the page, and a copy of your report is emailed to you. No card is required.
How is my FLOS Readiness Score calculated?
Your answers are scored across four pillars — Flow, Load, Output and Sustain. Each pillar is normalized to 25 points for a total score out of 100. Your lowest pillar is identified as the weakest link your effort is leaking through, and your verdict and recommendation follow from that score.
Are the calorie and protein targets medical advice?
No. Targets are population-level estimates from the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and standard activity multipliers, with protein derived from published sports-nutrition meta-analyses. [1][2][3][4] They are educational starting points to refine with weekly reviews — not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Consult a physician or registered dietitian before changing your diet or training.
What is the FLOS Protocol?
FLOS Protocol is a 12-week self-coaching command system for fitness: a protected tracking app, guided launch plan, weekly review tools, macro and RPE references, and a 90-day scorecard — organized into Core, Pro and Elite tiers. This free audit is a working sample of its tracking-and-review method.
