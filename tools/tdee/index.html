How this number is computed

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation estimates your resting metabolism from the four variables that drive it. In a systematic review of predictive equations, it came within 10% of measured values in more adults than Harris-Benedict, Owen, or the WHO formulas — which is why dietetic literature treats it as the default starting estimate. [1][2]

Men: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

Why maintenance is a draft, not a verdict

Every calculator — this one included — produces a population-level estimate. Two people with identical stats can differ by hundreds of calories a day. That is not a reason to distrust numbers; it is a reason to treat the number as version one and review it weekly against your own weight, waist, and performance trend. Adjust ±10% at a fixed weekly review and within 3–4 weeks your target is more accurate than any equation could make it.

Protein does the protective work in any phase: around 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day maximizes resistance-training gains in meta-analysis [3][4] — hit protein first, and let carbs and fats adjust around it. (Our protein target tool computes your exact grams.)

Questions, answered straight

Is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation accurate?

Within ~10% of measured resting metabolism for most adults — the best of the standard equations [1][2]. But "accurate for a population" still means "approximately right for you," which is exactly why your target deserves a weekly review instead of blind faith.

What deficit is best for fat loss?

~10–20% below maintenance (this tool's −15% default). Big 30–40% slashes cost lean mass, training quality, and eventually adherence — the slow cut wins because you can actually hold it.

Should I eat back exercise calories?

Not if your activity multiplier already includes your workouts — that's double-counting. Pick the activity level that honestly matches your week and leave it there.

How often should I recalculate?

After every 4–5 kg of weight change — or simply adjust ±10% at your weekly review based on your trend. Your own 4 weeks of records outrank any formula.
